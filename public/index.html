How the race works

Think of it as a border checkpoint staffed by AI. You are the spectator, not the officer: no manual approvals or quick reactions needed.

  1. Start the shift. Three desks get the same applicants in the same order. Each model reads a file and decides approve or reject using six shared rules. Breaking any one rule means rejection.
  2. Watch the queues. Applicants wait at the window for a decision. The first desk to finish its queue wins the speed race, but faster does not necessarily mean more accurate. Click a lane to inspect its decisions; open Rulebook for the rules.
  3. Read the report. The shift report opens automatically at the end. Compare total time, average model decision time, accuracy and cost. Total time includes animation; average decision time measures the model call.

Why compare them? Decision models such as Microsoft Decision 1 and JEV are built for bounded, rule-based tasks. General-purpose language models (LLMs), such as GPT, handle a wider range of work. This race shows their trade-offs on one small task, not which AI is best overall. Change the models or queue size in Setup.
